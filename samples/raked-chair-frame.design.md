# Raked chair frame — the design, as you would write it on paper

The frame of a chair whose rear legs rake back: the raked chair back of
[`docs/design/angled-parts.md`](../docs/design/angled-parts.md) §1.5. Each rear leg leans one way
only, so its two side faces stay square to the room, and a side rail can be held **flush** to its
inside face exactly (§3.2).

## Stated dimensions

| What | Dimension | Where it comes from |
|---|---|---|
| Rear legs | 2x2, 1 1/2" × 1 1/2" | the shipped library (PS 20-25 Table 3, dry) |
| Each rear leg | foot on the floor, top 24" up and 7" further back | design choice: a 7-24-25 triangle |
| Front legs | 2x2, 17" tall, 14" in front of the rear legs' feet | design choice |
| Side rails | 16" × 3" × 3/4", lying on edge 12" up | design choice |
| The chair | 16" wide outside the legs | design choice |

Both of each rear leg's ends are cut level (to the floor and a level top). Each side rail is held
flush to its rear leg's inside face. Its back end is mitred to the rake, 3" × 7/24 = 7/8" along its
bottom edge: a cut typed on the rail, which napkin does not keep matched to the rake if the rake
changes (#67).

## What the cut list should say

- **Rear leg**, 2, 2'-1 7/16" × 1 1/2" × 1 1/2", 2x2: 25" along the centreline plus half of each
  7/16" setback, exact; each end a mitre ≈16.5° off square.
- **Front leg**, 2, 1'-5" × 1 1/2" × 1 1/2", 2x2.
- **Side rail**, 2, 1'-4" × 3" × 3/4": the back end mitred 7/8" along the bottom edge, ≈16.5°.
