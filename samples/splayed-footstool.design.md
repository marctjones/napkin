# Splayed footstool — the design, as you would write it on paper

A small stool whose legs lean out two ways at once, like a sawhorse's: the worked example of
[`docs/design/angled-parts.md`](../docs/design/angled-parts.md) §9.2. The legs keep their wide
faces vertical (`reference: z`, the tool's default), so each end is still a single mitre — the
compound cut a magazine plan shows appears only when the wide face is kept parallel to a side
instead, which is the same four points and a different board (§9.2 board 2, in the tests).

## Stated dimensions

| What | Dimension | Where it comes from |
|---|---|---|
| Seat | 12" × 12" × 3/4" plywood | design choice; 3/4 plywood is 3/4" (PS 1-19 Table 10, the shipped library) |
| Seat underside | 12" above the floor | design choice |
| Legs | 2x2, 1 1/2" × 1 1/2" | the shipped library (PS 20-25 Table 3, dry) |
| Each leg's top | 3" in from both seat edges | design choice |
| Each leg's foot | 3" further out in X and 4" further out in Y than its top | design choice: 3² + 4² + 12² = 13² |

## Relationships

The seat is anchored; each leg's top is held to its underside and edges, and each foot to its own
top: 3" out in X, 4" out in Y, 12" lower.

Each leg's top is butted to the seat's underside with pocket screws drilled from the leg's bottom
face, and glued — the classic splayed-leg attachment (angled-parts §5).

## What the cut list should say

- **Leg**, 4, 1'-1 5/8" × 1 1/2" × 1 1/2", 2x2: a 13" centreline plus half of each 5/8" setback,
  exact. Each end is mitred ≈22.5° off square. Drill 2 pocket holes in the top (east) end from the
  bottom face: the top meets the seat over 1 1/2" × 1 5/8", and pocket screws are at least 2, one per
  2". Eight pocket screws in all, and glue on four joints.
- **Seat**, 1, 1'-0" × 1'-0" × 3/4", 3/4 plywood.
